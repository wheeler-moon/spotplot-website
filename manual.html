SpotPlot Manual

Everything you need to go from an empty show to printed spot paperwork. New to SpotPlot? Tips also appear the first time you open each screen, and Help → Show Tips Again brings them back.

Install and sign in

  1. Download SpotPlot and open SpotPlot.dmg.
  2. Read the license agreement and click Agree.
  3. Drag SpotPlot into your Applications folder, then open it from there.
  4. Enter your email address and the license key from your purchase email, then click Sign In. This step needs an internet connection. Your name comes with your license. The first time, SpotPlot welcomes you and offers to create your first show.

SpotPlot needs a Mac with Apple silicon (M1 or newer). SpotPlot updates itself: while a new version downloads, a small notice in the corner says so, and SpotPlot asks before installing it. You can also choose SpotPlot → Check for Updates… to check right away; it tells you if you're already up to date.

Your name is at the top right of the Home screen; click it to open Settings, which shows your account: your name (click Edit to change it, or Add if there isn't one), plan, status and license key (with a Copy button). It's where you turn automatic update checks on or off, check for updates, contact support or send feedback. Moving to a new Mac? Click Sign Out… next to This Mac first; your shows stay on the old Mac, so export any you want to bring along.

How SpotPlot works

You set up a show once: its spots (with their operators and gels), its scenes and its characters. Then you build the cue list, where each light cue is a row and each spot has a column. Everything you print comes from the cue list, so your paperwork is always current.

  1. Create the show and its spots.
  2. Add scenes and characters.
  3. Build the cue list.
  4. Print spot sheets, the caller sheet, the color load, spot notes and the cast sheet.

Create a show

On the Home screen, click + New Show (or press ⌘N).

  • Show info: title (the only required field), theatre, producer and an optional show logo. The logo prints on every sheet.
  • Lighting team: lighting designer, associate, assistant, production electrician and programmer. Their names appear in the page headers.
  • Spots: a show can have up to 4 spots. See the next section.

Click Save Show. You can change any of this later in Show Settings.

Spots and color frames

For each spot, set the operator name, location (like "FOH Left") and fixture type (pick from the list, or choose Other and type it).

Color frames

Each spot starts with six color frames, F1 to F6. Fewer on your fixture? Hover a frame and click the small red × to remove it, and + Add frame to bring one back. Frames keep their numbers and their place: removing Frame 5 leaves an empty Frame 5 slot between F4 and F6 on the cue list, the dashboard and prints, and Spot Settings can add it back. If cues use the frame you're removing, SpotPlot asks whether to switch them to another frame or take it out of them. Type a gel number or name in a frame to search over 1,100 gels from Rosco (Roscolux, Cinegel, CalColor and E-Colour+), Lee and GAM, and pick the gel. Swatches show each gel's real color. Using a gel that isn't listed? Just type it: it's kept exactly as typed and shows as clear/white. Need two gels in one frame? Click + Add gel under it: the frame prints as, for example, R02+R132, and the color load shows each gel as a band of its color.

  • Permanent color: tick it for a gel that always stays in the fixture, then pick the gel. It shows as PERM on the sheets.
  • Copy colors from…: same gel load on every spot? Fill in one spot, then use this menu on the others to copy all of its frames, its permanent color and its fixture type.

The show dashboard

The Home screen lists your shows with the one you changed most recently first, and when each was last modified ("Modified today at 3:42 PM", "Modified 5 days ago"). Opening or looking through a show doesn't count as a change. Use the two buttons at the top right of your shows to switch between the grid and a list.

Click a show on the Home screen to open its dashboard. From here you reach the Cue List, Scenes, Characters, Spot Notes and Print. The dashboard also lists the spots and lighting team.

  • Show Settings opens every setting for the show (see Show Settings).
  • Export ▾ saves the whole show as a .spotplot file, or as a spreadsheet (see Sharing).
  • iPad Sync opens this show's iPad settings (the iPad app for operators is coming soon). Its dots show how many iPads are connected to the show right now: one green dot per iPad, or a number when there are more than 6. Grey means none.
  • Spots shows each spot's operator, position and color load, its frames lined up in columns. Click a spot to edit it.

Scenes

Scenes break the show into sections. They become the green headers in the cue list and the black bars on your prints.

  • Add a scene with a label (like "Scene 1"), a song, or both.
  • Mark a scene as an act break and it shows in orange. Act breaks always show in the cue list and on spot and caller sheets, even with no cues of their own; other scenes show once they have cues.
  • Drag a scene's handle to put scenes in show order, and Edit to change one.

You can also add a scene from the cue list with + Scene.

Characters

Add each character with the performer's name, costume notes and a photo (click the photo box, or drop an image on it). Characters fill the character menu in every cue.

  • The Print checkbox decides who appears on the printed cast sheet.
  • Drag the handle to reorder; Edit changes a character.
  • For a pickup of more than one character, like "Rizzo & Danny", add each to the cue with the + beside its character (see below).

Photos are copied into SpotPlot, so moving or deleting the original file won't break them.

The cue list

Open the cue list from the dashboard. Each row is one light cue; each spot has a column.

Adding cues

  • + Add cue at end at the bottom of the list, or press ⌘=.
  • To add a cue between two others, hover between them and click + insert cue here. To make a new first cue for a scene (or a cue under an act break), hover just below the scene's header and click it there.
  • A new cue picks up where each spot left off: spots that were on keep their characters and color; spots that faded out start as Off.

The cue column

  • Click the number to type the LQ (the light cue number).
  • T· is the spot cue number operators use to keep their place. It's printed under each LQ.
  • Click pg next to T· to note the script page the cue is on. It's just for you: it shows on the cue list, not on prints.
  • The menu under the number sets the cue's scene. Delete removes the cue.

Renumber T·

Inserting cues makes T· numbers jump around. Click Renumber T· at the top of the cue list to number them 1, 2, 3… from top to bottom. Your cue order, scenes and LQ numbers never change. If you've already handed out sheets, reprint them afterward so everyone's T· numbers match. Changed your mind? Press ⌘Z to put the old numbers back.

Getting around

Use Jump to to go straight to a scene. Scene headers stay pinned at the top while you scroll; a [bracketed] header means you're partway through that scene. Double-click a scene header to rename the scene or its song right there. Leave the cue list and come back, and it opens where you left off. Escape closes any menu or pop-up.

Want a spot's gel load in view while you work? Click the little color button at the right of the spot's name at the top of its column. Its frames (and permanent color) stay listed under the name until you click it again, even if you leave the cue list and come back.

Filling in a cue

Each spot's box in a cue holds what that spot does.

FieldWhat it's for
ActionClick to choose: Pick Up, Increase, Decrease, Fade Out, Fade In Place, Bump Up, Bump Out, Swap To, Slide To, Stay With, Iris In, Iris Out, Iris/Fade Up, Iris/Fade Down, Iris/Fade Out, Up & Out, Dump & Restore, Bump Color, Roll Color, Ballyhoo, Off, or Tracked. Some actions fill in a starting intensity and time for you. Changing a cue to Tracked clears it (time, intensity, When, w/LQ, notes) and carries on from that spot's cue above: the same character, iris size and color frames. It keeps following: change the iris or color above and the Tracked cues below change too. Change a character and the new name follows that person down to their fade out, stopping at Off, a new Pick Up, Swap To or Slide To, or a cue on someone else.

Tick "Remember my choice for this show" to stop being asked; View → Ask About New Characters Again turns the question back on. Click + beside the character to add a second, then a third ("Pick Up Bob & Matt"); × takes one out. Prints and the iPad show them all.
IntensityFull, a percentage, Glow or Out. Prints as "@ Full".
TimeFade time in seconds, or Custom.
IrisFB (Full Body), 3/4, 1/2, H&S (Head & Shoulders), Hd (Head), plus any sizes you add, in the order set in Show Settings → Iris Sizes. Click again to clear.
ColorClick the frames in use (F1, F2…); two frames print as F1+2. NC means no color. Hover a frame to see its gel. Lit frames show bright when the spot's color changes in that cue and muted when it's the same as before, so color changes stand out. Printed sheets do the same: a change is highlighted in yellow and an unchanged color prints grey.
WhenThe cue's "go" moment, like "Nora at the window". Turn on w/LQ to make it read "w/ LQ" plus this cue's number; it stays linked if you renumber.
NotesAnything else for the operator.

Off means the spot isn't used in that cue. Click + Note in an Off box to leave the operator a note while their spot is off, like where to move next or something to set up in the dark. It prints across the row on their spot sheet and under Off on the caller sheet, and if you later give that cue an action, the note becomes its regular Notes line. Changing a cue to Off clears its note, so an Off box starts empty. Tracked clears the cue so the spot simply carries on from before. On the spot sheets and caller sheet, a cue where a spot carries on prints without an action, in grey, so the cues that need the operator stand out.

In When and Notes, select text and press ⌘B, ⌘I or ⌘U for bold, italic or underline. Press ⌘Z to undo.

Highlights, notes and more

Double-click any spot's box in a cue to open its options. (Inside When or Notes, double-click selects a word instead.)

  • Highlight the whole cue, or just its When or Notes line, in yellow or red. Highlights print too.
  • Ignore cue crosses the cue out without deleting it.
  • Spot note: a note for the operator. A yellow dot marks cues that have one, and they collect on the Spot Notes screen.

Drag to copy or swap

Drag one spot's box onto another to copy it (with or without the character), or swap the two.

Right-click a cue

Right-click a spot's box for a menu of quick changes:

  • Copy Cue and Paste Cue: copy what a spot does in one cue into another spot or cue. Paste Cue Without Character keeps the character that's already there. Frames the other spot doesn't have are left out.
  • Highlight: yellow or red for the whole cue, the When line or the Notes line. Choose a checked color again to take it off.
  • Ignore Cue and Add Spot Note…, the same as in the double-click box.
  • Insert Cue Above, Insert Cue Below and Delete the cue (for every spot).
  • Clear Cue empties the box so you can start that cue from scratch (a spot that's Off stays Off).
  • Copy Whole Cue (All Spots) and Paste Whole Cue copy what every spot does in one cue into another cue.

Right-click a cue's LQ number for the same whole-cue copy and paste, plus Insert Cue Above / Below and Delete.

Press ⌘Z to undo any change in the cue list: an action (with the time and intensity it filled in), a character, a paste, highlight, ignore or clear, adding, inserting or deleting a cue, checking off a note, or renaming a scene. ⇧⌘Z redoes it (or use Edit → Undo and Redo). While you're typing in a box, they undo and redo your typing.

Spot notes

The Spot Notes screen shows the notes still to give, grouped by spot (with an All Spots column when there are notes for everyone) and in cue order. Each card shows the LQ, the scene, what the spot is doing and the note. Once you've given a note, click ✓ Given: it leaves the cue (so its yellow dot goes away) and moves to the Given tray at the bottom of the screen. Open the tray to see every given note, newest first; Restore puts one back on its cue. Edit or delete notes here too. Go to Cue opens the cue list at that cue, with the spot's box outlined. Print them from the Print screen.

Taking notes during a run

In the cue list, click the notes button (a page and pen, at the far right of the top bar) to open the notes sidebar on the right. Type the LQ, pick the spot, write the note and press Return; it's saved as that cue's spot note, so the Spot Notes screen and prints have it too. Pick All Spots for a note everyone needs: it gets its own column on the Spot Notes screen and prints at the top of every spot's notes. The sidebar lists the notes you haven't given yet, in cue order (filter by spot): click one to jump to its cue, or ✓ to check it off. The yellow number on the notes button counts notes still to give.

Printing

Open Print from the dashboard and pick what to print. The preview on the right is the real PDF, page breaks and all, and it updates as you change options. Click Export PDF… to save it.

Page Setup… (or ⌘P) sets the paper (Letter or Legal), the orientation, and whether to print all pages, a page range, or a T· range of cues. Cue Details there chooses which details print (the same boxes as Cue Settings). Spot sheets print portrait and the caller sheet landscape. Print remembers your choices for each show.

Cover page (caller and spot sheets) adds a bordered first page: the label, the show name and a large logo, the theatre and producer, each spot's number, operator, position and fixture with its color load (just that spot's on a spot sheet; on the caller sheet every spot's frames line up in the same columns), a key to the action symbols the show uses, and the lighting team along the bottom. Every sheet has a Label field for the page header (like "2-24 Dress Run") and "Page 1 of N" at the bottom. Color changes are highlighted in yellow, including each spot's first color, and a cue never splits across two pages.

Sharing and backups

Export a show

On the dashboard click Export ▾ and choose SpotPlot Show File… (or press ⌘E) to save a .spotplot file. It holds the entire show: spots, gels, scenes, characters, every cue, notes, highlights, photos and the logo. Use it to send the show to your associate or another designer, or as a backup.

Export as a spreadsheet

Choose Export ▾ → Spreadsheet (CSV)… (or File → Export as Spreadsheet (CSV)…) to save a folder of CSV files that open in Excel, Numbers or Google Sheets:

  • Cue List: one row per cue, with each spot's action, character, intensity, iris, frames, gels, time, When and Notes side by side.
  • Spot Cues: one row for each spot in each cue, easy to sort and filter (everything Spot 2 does, every Pickup).
  • Spots, Scenes, Characters and Spot Notes.

Names are spelled out (characters, scenes, gels), and everything is included, even details hidden in Cue Settings. A spreadsheet is a copy for reading and sharing; to move a show to another Mac, use the .spotplot file.

Import a show

Double-click a .spotplot file, or click Import ▾ on the Home screen and choose SpotPlot Show File… (or press ⌘I). The show is added alongside your others, so nothing is overwritten.

Import a show from FileMaker

Bring a show over from a FileMaker followspot database. You don't need FileMaker.

  1. Click Import ▾ on the Home screen and choose FileMaker Show… (or File → Import from FileMaker…).
  2. Choose the show's FS_Track.fmp12. If SceneList.fmp12 is in the same folder, the scene list (with act breaks) comes too.
  3. SpotPlot shows what it found. If the show uses actions or iris sizes that aren't exactly SpotPlot's (like "Intensity Up" or "HB"), pick what each one means, or keep it as a custom action or size.
  4. Click Import Show. The show opens, ready to work on.

What comes across: the show's details and lighting team, each spot's operator, position and gels (with the permanent color), characters with performers and costume notes, scenes, and every cue with its LQ, T· number, page and what each spot does. A spot with no action that's still on its character carries on (no action, the same as a cue you insert and leave alone); OFF becomes Off. The file's per-cue location goes in the cue's Notes line. Names typed in capitals become Title Case (ANNIE → Annie).

Not included: the logo, performer photos and the file's action pictures. Add those in SpotPlot. Your FileMaker file is only read, never changed. An encrypted FileMaker file can't be read; open it in FileMaker Pro and save an unencrypted copy first.

Shows are saved automatically on your Mac as you work. Exporting a copy before a big change, or at the end of each day of tech, is an easy backup.
